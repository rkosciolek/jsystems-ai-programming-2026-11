# WS4 - Reasoning / logic / math / knowledge benchmarks by language, and "language of thought"

Date of research: 2026-11-24. Scope: primary sources only. Items marked UNVERIFIED-SECONDARY came from search-engine snippets or aggregators and were not confirmed in a primary document.

## 1) Executive summary

- Language does measurably affect reasoning accuracy, but the effect is task-, model- and language-dependent. It is largest for low-resource languages (Swahili, Telugu, Yoruba) and for long multi-step math when the model is forced to reason in the target language.
- For strong models on knowledge/MCQ benchmarks, Slavic gaps are small. MMLU-ProX (Table 3, 5-shot CoT) shows most frontier-class models within about 0-4 points of English for Czech, Ukrainian and Serbian. Russian shows the largest gap in several models (up to about 12 points for o4-mini).
- Polish is NOT in MMLU-ProX, MMLU-ProX Lite, OpenAI MMMLU, or Saji/Tam et al. language sets. The only 2026 primary source that measures a Polish-vs-English reasoning gap is PluraMath (arXiv 2607.05992, July 2026): average high-resource vs Polish gap of +0.67 points (one of the smallest in the study). The table is partly garbled, so confidence is MEDIUM.
- No primary source found showing Polish reasoning is better than English. The "Polish beats English" claim rests on OneRuler (long-context retrieval, WS1 scope), not on reasoning.
- Reasoning-language effects: reasoning in English usually gives higher accuracy than forced native-language reasoning (Tam et al. 2025; Barua et al. ICLR 2026), but native reasoning also has a cost in readability and oversight. Several papers attribute most of the gap to comprehension (Kang et al. 2026), not to reasoning capacity.
- Most primary evidence is 2025 and uses older models (o4-mini, GPT-4.1, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, QwQ, R1-Distill). Newest per-language data found: GPT-5.4 and Claude Haiku 4.5 in PluraMath (2026). No per-language table for Gemini 3.x, Claude 4.x/5.x Sonnet/Opus, Qwen3.5+ or DeepSeek V4 was found.

## 3) Synthesis: does language affect reasoning level?

Evidence that language matters:
1. Non-English accuracy is lower on average for most models. On MMLU-ProX the largest gaps are for RU and for older/non-reasoning models (o4-mini RU -11.7; GPT-4.1 RU -8.6; Llama 3.3-70B UK -5.8).
2. Forcing reasoning in a low-resource language costs the most (Swahili MATH-500 -31.5 pts; Target-CoT AIME in Swahili/Thai/Latvian/Telugu 25-45 pts below English-CoT for R1-distill-70B). Slavic languages were not in these low-resource tests.
3. Multi-step tasks widen the gap (Barua et al.: gap larger for long reasoning such as math). PolyMath: thinking-trace language consistency is low for reasoning models, and it correlates negatively with accuracy at high difficulty (paper's correlation, not causation).
4. Models default to English (or Chinese) as their internal reasoning language (Tam et al.; Wendler et al.; Schut et al.). This is an English-centric "concept space" claim.

Evidence against a large effect for Slavic languages on capable models:
1. MMLU-ProX Table 3: DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B-Think are within 0-4 points of English for RU, UK, CS and SR; Czech is within 0.2-2.7 points for several models.
2. PolyMath Table 5a: Russian is equal to or better than English for DeepSeek-R1, o1-mini, Gemini-2.5-pro (within 1 point) in the hardest subset.
3. PluraMath (July 2026): Polish has one of the smallest high-vs-target gaps (+0.67 pts); Polish is not a strong outlier for underrepresentation.
4. Kang et al. (2026): much of the gap is in understanding the input, and translating about 20% of it closes most of the gap. This suggests the gap is a comprehension/translation step rather than a reasoning deficit. Fixing comprehension (e.g., translating the prompt first) is a practical lever.

Mechanisms (ranked by evidence strength):
- Comprehension and translation of the prompt (Kang 2026; PluraMath) - MEDIUM.
- English-pivot latent space (Wendler 2024; Schut 2025; Anthropic 2025 shared features) - qualitative, MEDIUM.
- Training-data imbalance and tokenizer fragmentation (UA-Legal snippet; PolyMath) - MEDIUM/LOW.
- Reasoning-trace language drift in RL-trained reasoning models (PolyMath; Tam) - MEDIUM.

Statistical meaning: on MMLU-ProX with thousands of items per language, a 1-point gap is likely above noise, but on the PluraMath per-language rows (small item counts, one run) a gap of 0.67 pts is within noise. Treat per-language differences below ~2 points as not meaningful without repeated runs or CIs.

Answer to the team question (WS4 scope): Language does change measured reasoning accuracy, mostly by a few points for strong models on Slavic languages and by tens of points for low-resource languages or when forced to reason in them. No primary 2026 source shows that Polish or any Slavic language produces better reasoning than English. The honest 2026 conclusion for Polish: small or no reasoning penalty for strong models, a larger penalty for older models and for multi-step math if the reasoning trace is in Polish. "Polish beats English" is not supported by reasoning evidence.

Like-for-like caveats: MMLU-ProX, PolyMath, PluraMath and Tam/Barua use parallel or translated items (like-for-like for the same question). LLMzSzL, PLCC, Bielik and ZNO-Eval use native-language items only (not like-for-like). Polish medical LEK comparison used translated counterparts from a different study (not like-for-like across models).
